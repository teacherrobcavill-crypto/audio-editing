3 What Is Happening? (Wave Superposition & Comb Filtering)

Understanding the AoS 2 physics behind the visualiser above earns full marks in Component 4 written analysis.

1. Propagation Delay ($t = d / 343$)

Electricity travels down a guitar cable at near the speed of light (effectively 0 ms delay). Sound travels from the amp speaker cone to the microphone at roughly 343 m/s.

  • A mic placed 51 cm (0.51 m) away arrives 0.51 / 343 = 1.49 ms (~72 samples at 48 kHz) late.
  • A mic placed 1.71 m away arrives 5.00 ms (~240 samples) late.

2. Frequency-Dependent Comb Filtering

A fixed time delay affects different frequencies by different phase angles:

  • At 100 Hz (period = 10 ms), a 5 ms delay is half a cycle (180°) → total cancellation.
  • At 200 Hz (period = 5 ms), the same 5 ms delay is a full cycle (360°) → constructive reinforcement.
  • This alternating pattern of deep notches and peaks looks like a hair comb on an analyser — hence comb filtering.
Concept Polarity Inversion (Ø / 180°) Time Alignment (Sample Nudging / Delay)
What it does Flips positive voltage to negative (and vice versa) across all frequencies equally. Shifts an entire audio region forward or backward in time by exact milliseconds/samples.
When to use it When a mic/speaker/DI transformer is wired 180° opposite (waves are mirror images at 0 ms). When one mic is physically further from the source than another (or DI vs. mic'd amp).
Why polarity alone isn't enough Flipping polarity on a 5 ms delayed track fixes cancellation at 100 Hz, but creates a new cancellation notch at 200 Hz! Only time alignment aligns all harmonics simultaneously.

4 Why Is It Best to Phase-Align Audio?

Four practical and sonic reasons examiners look for when you justify phase alignment in Section B.

1. Restores Low-End Weight (+6 dB Coherent Sum)

When the DI sub-fundamental and Amp fundamental peak together in phase, their amplitudes sum constructively (+6 dB), giving solid, consistent bass weight across every note on the fretboard.

2. Eliminates Hollow Midrange Notches

Removing comb filtering clears out boxy/nasal dips in the 200–800 Hz range, preserving the natural pick attack and woody fret definition of the bass.

3. Stops "Fix-It-With-EQ" Headroom Loss

Boosting +8 dB at 80 Hz on an EQ cannot restore frequencies that were cancelled by phase. Aligning phase first prevents muddy EQ boosts and keeps downstream compressors reacting accurately.

4. Preserves Transient Punch & Mono Compatibility

Aligned DI + Amp transients hit simultaneously instead of smearing ("flamming"), keeping the groove locked to the kick drum and ensuring the bass translates cleanly on mono club/PA systems.

5 Component 4 Exam Practice

Question (AO2/AO3 — Bass DI + Amp Alignment) 5 marks

A bass guitar is recorded simultaneously via a DI box and a dynamic microphone placed 68 cm from the amplifier speaker. When both faders are raised, the bass sounds thin and lacks sub-bass weight. Explain why this occurs and describe how to fix it in a DAW without using EQ.

Show Mark Scheme
  • The DI signal arrives almost instantaneously, whereas the microphone signal is delayed by acoustic propagation (~2 ms at 68 cm) (1).
  • Summing the two signals creates frequency-dependent phase differences, causing destructive interference / comb filtering and low-frequency cancellation (1).
  • Zoom in to sample level on both waveforms and nudge/delay the DI track later (or nudge the mic track earlier) so the first positive transient peaks align (2).
  • Check 180° polarity inversion (Ø) on one track and keep whichever polarity gives the fullest low-end response (1).
