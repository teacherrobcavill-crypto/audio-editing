Snare Top vs. Bottom: When the drumstick hits the top skin, it pushes the top mic diaphragm down/away while pushing the bottom skin toward the bottom mic — creating natural 180° opposite polarity!

2 Key Exam Takeaways (AoS 1 & AoS 2)

Snare Top vs. Snare Bottom

Because the two microphones face opposite sides of the drum, their initial transients move in opposite directions (180° out of polarity). Inverting the polarity (Ø) on the bottom snare restores the 200 Hz fundamental punch without needing time delay.

Close Mics vs. Overheads (3:1 Rule)

Overheads sit 1–2 metres above the kit, so snare sound arrives 3–6 ms later than the close snare mic. Nudging the close mic later (or aligning transients) stops comb filtering and keeps stereo imaging tight.

Quick Exam Check (3 Marks): Why invert polarity on a bottom snare mic?
  • The top and bottom snare mics face opposite directions on either side of the drum shell (1).
  • A downward drumstick strike pushes air away from the top mic and toward the bottom mic, producing waveforms 180° out of polarity (1).
  • Inverting polarity (Ø) aligns the positive/negative peaks, preventing low-mid cancellation and restoring body/punch (1).
