2 Theory & Practical Problems of Retuning Vocals

Examiners test both how pitch correction works (AoS 2) and how to avoid classic production mistakes (AoS 1 / AO3).

1. Robotic / Stair-Stepping Artifacts

Setting Retune Speed to 100% (0 ms) and Pitch Drift to 0% strips away natural pitch scoops, glides, and vibrato. The vocal snaps rigidly to semitones, creating an artificial, robotic timbre.

2. Formant Shift ("Chipmunk" / "Monster")

When transposing a vocal by 3+ semitones (e.g. creating harmonies), shifting pitch without Formant Preservation scales the vocal tract resonances upward/downward, changing the singer's perceived throat size.

3. Sibilance & Breath Warbling

Unvoiced consonants (s, t, f, sh) and breaths contain broadband noise with no clear fundamental pitch. Letting Flex Pitch/Melodyne track them causes metallic warbling — always split and bypass sibilants/breaths.

4. Monophonic Limitation & Scale Lock

Standard pitch trackers require a monophonic signal (single note at a time); reverb tails or bleed can confuse pitch detection. Always set the correct key/scale so passing notes aren't pulled out of key.

Component 4 Exam Practice (5 Marks): A student tunes a backing vocal +4 semitones and sets Drift to 0%. Evaluate the result.
  • With Drift at 0% and fast retune, natural vibrato and pitch transitions are removed, making the vocal sound robotic/unnatural (1).
  • If Formant Preservation is off, a +4 semitone shift raises vocal tract formants, creating an unnatural "chipmunk" timbre (1); enabling Formant Preservation maintains natural character (1).
  • Unvoiced consonants/sibilants may exhibit metallic artifacts if not excluded from pitch processing (1).
  • Recommend moderate retune speed, retaining natural drift/vibrato, and verifying harmonic blend against the lead vocal (1).
